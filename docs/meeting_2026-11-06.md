## 5a. Why policies drift out of the zone late in training
- In the kinematic runs, PPO's KL limit aborts almost every update after ~15M steps (73–98% stop in the
  first epoch), and the critic explains almost nothing (explained variance 0.01–0.13).
- So learning is nearly frozen apart from a little noisy drift. Because the delivery is knife-edge, that
  drift alone can lose the zone (a250 seed 1: 76% → 5% good in the last 5M steps).
- The same brittleness to timing error makes the policy brittle to its own parameter drift.
- Fix tested today: larger PPO minibatches (4096 instead of 64). Every update now runs all 10 epochs
  (before: 0.3 of 10). The run was cut at 10M by low laptop memory, so it isn't known yet whether a250
  seed 1 keeps the zone. The critic is still poor: a second problem.

## 5b. Link to the cricket papers he sent
- **Lindsay et al. 2024** (J Sports Sci 42:708, swing bowling, elite Australian fast bowlers): the
  pelvis and trunk stay stable from delivery to delivery, and the bowling arm varies. That arm
  variation is functional: it sets the seam for swing.
  - Our full-body bowler has this structure by construction: the body is the fixed capture and only
    the arm varies. So the question is whether the arm's variation is functional. Under torque noise
    it is, but only weakly (covariation 1.05–1.37).
  - Caveat: the simulation has no seam, swing, wrist or fingers, which is what Lindsay's arm variation
    is for.
- **Andrews et al. 2024** (Eur J Sport Sci, 92 fast bowlers): delivery-stride timing varies more than
  delivery speed, so bowlers vary their timing to keep speed consistent.
  - Same pattern in our bowler. Under torque noise, the arm's timing (horizontal → release) varies
    1.6–3.4× more than release speed, in every policy (best policy: CV 0.044 vs 0.027).
  - Caveat: a CV comparison shows the pattern, not that the timing variation *causes* the steady speed.
    Andrews infers it the same way.
  - Our captured bowler doesn't show it: across 11 deliveries, run-up timing CV is 0.03–0.05 and speed CV
    is 0.04–0.07. But it's one non-elite bowler on a short indoor run-up, with release speed for only 4
    deliveries, so this is a limitation, not a finding. Better human data would need more deliveries
    with the hand tracked through release.
