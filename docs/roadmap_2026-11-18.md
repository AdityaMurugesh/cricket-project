# Roadmap: methods that would change the project, not tune it (18 Nov 2026)

The user's verdict on 18 Nov: "I'm not really seeing improvements." That is fair. From 23 Oct to 5 Nov
every step was "train a PPO batch with one knob changed, score it" (noise, batch size, speed weight,
speed power). Each answered a small question and none moved the headline. This file lists what could,
ranked by how much it changes the result versus what it costs. Status is updated as items get built.

**Compute rule:** the user's laptop must not lag. Heavy work goes to the HPC. Local runs are short,
single-process tests only. HPC state on 18 Nov: cpuq completely full (all 64-core nodes busy), a cap of
12 queued+running jobs per user, and H100/H200 GPU nodes in gpuq (see item 15).

## 0. What the literature says about our result (research done 18 Nov)

| Finding | Source | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Release-timing precision needed for accurate throws ≈ 1 ms; the launch window shrinks ~8× when distance doubles | Calvin 1983, J Theor Biol ("A stone's throw and its launch window") | The same law as our landing-slope ∝ speed^2.86 result |
| Optimal simulated over-arm throws to 6–8 m targets have a launch window of ~1.2 ms | Chowdhary & Challis 1999 (simulation) | Our 102 km/h delivery is good for ~1.2 ms (slope 17 m per 10 ms against a 2 m zone). Same number |
| Real release-timing SD is 3–15 ms depending on the task; skilled amateurs ~±10 ms; pitchers estimated to need 1–2 ms | Hore et al. 1995, Exp Brain Res; Hore 1996; review in PMC3922883 | Our 12 ms jitter is at the unskilled end. It is the right model for a non-elite bowler and too large for an elite one. This answers the supervisor question "is 12 ms realistic?" |
| In darts, timing is not the main limit on precision | Smeets, Frens & Brenner 2002, Exp Brain Res | A counterpoint: at low speed other noise dominates |
| A 16-segment torque-driven simulation of elite fast bowlers, optimised for maximum ball speed | Felton, King, Yeadon et al. (Loughborough, 2017–2020) | The closest prior work. It optimises a single deterministic delivery, not a noise-robust one. **Our gap: noise-robust (equifinal) optimisation, with legality** |
| Elbow hyperextension recoil can add ~5% ball speed | Felton et al. 2016, J Sports Sci | Our bowlers all sit 18–22° hyperextended. Is that a real speed source or a model artefact? (item 9) |
| Thoracic kinetic energy, upper-arm circumduction speed, forearm KE and pelvis–shoulder separation explain 55% of the variance in ball speed | Sports Technology 2012, DOI 10.1080/19346182.2012.737800 | Pace comes from the kinetic chain. The arm alone (our model) caps it (item 13) |
| Physics-based sports agents learned from broadcast video (tennis) | Zhang et al. 2023 SIGGRAPH, "Vid2Player3D" | A route to elite technique data without a lab (item 16) |
| Muscle-driven arm model in MuJoCo, 63 muscle-tendon units, MJX/Warp support | MyoSuite / MyoArm (Caggiano et al. 2022) | A realistic replacement for torque motors (item 14) |
| MJX/Warp: a humanoid at ~0.65–3.3M steps/s on one GPU | MuJoCo docs (MJX) | ~1000× our CPU throughput (item 15) |
| Release timing is the main obstacle in RL throwing; DartBot learns its release | arXiv 2509.23721 (DA-MMP), DartBot | Learned release is open and hard (item 3) |

**New framing for the report:** "Fast bowling is a launch-window problem. With a legal arm and a fixed
actuator budget, we compute the trade-off between pace and timing tolerance by noise-robust trajectory
optimisation, show that it reproduces the throwing literature's ~1 ms launch window at ~100 km/h, and
test whether RL can reach it." Item 1 makes this the headline.

## Tier 1: build now (high impact, cheap)

### 1. Pace/tolerance frontier across actuator budgets (the project's original deliverable)
- The original research question (Sept) was the speed/legality/accuracy trade-off across 2–3 actuator
  budgets. `scripts/kin_frontier.py` computes it directly. Add `--torque-scale` and `--top-speed` so each
  budget gets its own curve.
- Status: v1 (coarse knots) done for a270. v2 (a knot per decision, exact PPO warm starts) built, moved
  to the HPC (`hpc/frontier.pbs`).

### 2. Exact tolerance in the reward (an "equifinality reward")
- Today, noise training samples one jitter per delivery and pays 0/1. That is high variance. The env can
  instead compute the landing curve over ±40 ms at release and pay the exact expected good rate under
  N(0, σ): `p_jit`, the measure we already score with.
- Same objective, far lower variance, and no luck in the signal. Expected: faster and more reliable noise
  training. Flag `--tolerance-reward`.

### 3. The policy chooses its release (supervisor question 3)
- Today the ball goes when the arm passes a fixed angle. Add a release action: the ball goes when the
  policy's release output crosses 0. Jitter, if on, delays the actual release from that command. The
  release angle is then learned, not swept.
- Flag `--learned-release`.

### 4. One bowler, any length on demand (goal-conditioned)
- The target length (and line) is drawn per episode and given in the observation: yorker, full, good,
  short, bouncer. One policy bowls all of them. This is a visible demo, and it tests equifinality across
  goals.
- Flag `--target-sample lo hi` (zone half-width 1 m around a sampled centre).

### 5. Muscle-like torque–velocity limit (replaces linear damping)
- Today the arm's speed cap is viscous damping = peak torque / 40 rad/s, a modelling choice that sets the
  102 km/h. Real muscle follows Hill's force–velocity curve: available torque falls as joint speed rises,
  zero at ω_max. `T_avail(ω) = T0 * (1 - ω/ω_max) / (1 + ω/(k ω_max))` for concentric motion, with k ≈ 0.25.
- Report every frontier under both the old and the Hill model. Flag `--actuator hill`, `--omega-max`.

### 6. PPO started from optimised deliveries (behaviour cloning)
- `scripts/bc_from_frontier.py` (built). It needs the arm handover (built) so that one plan fits every
  start. It runs after item 1 on the HPC: clone each τ's optimum, then fine-tune with timing noise.

## Tier 2: next (medium effort)

### 7. Train across the captured deliveries
- References exist for T1a, T2b, T3a and T4b (the HPC has only T3a and T4b, so copy the other two). Each
  episode draws a body. Do the policies generalise to a delivery they never saw?

### 8. A real ball flight: drag, spin (Magnus) and seam swing, plus the bounce
- Today the flight is a drag-free parabola. Add quadratic drag (Cd ≈ 0.4–0.5) and a lift/side force from
  spin and seam angle (Mehta 1985, 2008; Grimshaw et al. 2024 review). This brings swing (Lindsay et al.
  2024) into reach: a seam-angle action and swing as a goal.

### 9. The elbow: fix the joint limit, then study hyperextension
- Every bowler sits 18–22° past the −15° limit (a soft limit pushed by the motor). Make the limit stiff and
  decide what ICC legality means for hyperextension recoil (Felton 2016: +5% speed). Re-run the frontier.

### 10. Wrist and finger flick
- The optimiser does not use the wrist (≈0 °/s at release). Real bowlers flex the wrist through release.
  Check the wrist actuator budget (50 N·m gear) and the grip-point model. Is the flick missing by design?

### 11. Closed-loop optimal control as a third reference (iLQR / optimal feedback control)
- Todorov & Jordan's minimum-intervention principle predicts which deviations a good controller corrects.
  An iLQR controller around the frontier trajectory gives the "ideal feedback" bowler to compare PPO with.
  This is the theory behind equifinality.

### 12. Signal-dependent noise in every actuator (Harris & Wolpert 1998)
- Torque noise exists (`torque_noise_sd`). Add it to the frontier's objective (a Monte Carlo p_good) so
  the curve answers "tolerant to timing AND motor noise".

## Tier 3: big swings (weeks)

### 13. Full-body physics bowler: pace from the kinetic chain
- The run-up and trunk replay the capture, so trunk speed is fixed. Pace in real bowlers comes from the
  chain (55% of speed variance, above). A physics body needs clean feet (the capture's feet skate 1 m/s:
  that is why stage 1 failed). Options: contact-aware retargeting, or learning only the last stride with
  the body free from front-foot contact (Felton's model starts at front-foot contact too).

### 14. A muscle-driven arm (MyoSuite MyoArm)
- 63 muscle-tendon units. Realistic speed limits, co-contraction, and signal-dependent noise come for
  free. It needs grafting onto our skeleton, or using MyoArm alone with the shoulder path prescribed.

### 15. GPU training (MJX or MuJoCo Warp) on the HPC's H100/H200 nodes
- ~1000× the throughput: thousands of seeds and full sweeps in minutes. KinematicBowlEnv is
  contact-free, which suits MJX well. It needs a JAX rewrite of the env step and a Brax PPO setup.

### 16. Elite technique from broadcast video (Vid2Player3D-style)
- Monocular pose estimation on broadcast fast bowling gives many elite bowlers, where we have one
  non-elite capture. Use it as the reference body for item 13.

### 17. A batter
- A simulated batter (or a scoring model by line/length/pace) turns "good length" into a strategic
  objective: variation, deception, the value of pace versus precision.

### 18. Better data from the lab
- Re-label in QTM (AIM failed), add a ball marker, track the hand through release, more bowlers, a radar
  gun for ball speed. It replaces the "hypothetical landing" of the real hand with a measured one.
