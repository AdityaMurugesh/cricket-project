# Pace vs timing tolerance: what the arm can do, not just what PPO found (18 Nov)

## Why this, and not another PPO batch
Since 23 Oct each step has been "train a PPO batch with one knob changed, score it": noise on/off,
batch size, speed weight, speed power. None of them could say whether the tolerant bowlers' 45–55 km/h
is a limit of the arm or a limit of PPO. This answers that directly.

## Method
- Same simulator, same scoring as every RL result: `KinematicBowlEnv` (captured body, torque-driven
  bowling arm), `kin_equifinality.delivery()` for the outcome and the exact good rate under N(0, 12 ms)
  release jitter (`p_jit`).
- Instead of a policy: open-loop arm torques (7 actuators, piecewise linear between knots every 40 ms
  through the arm circle), optimised with CMA-ES, warm-started from PPO policies.
- For each required tolerance τ: maximise release speed subject to legal (≤ 15°), good length
  (10.9–12.9 m), on line, and `p_jit ≥ τ`. Fixed release angle (250/270/290°, as the RL runs).
- `scripts/kin_frontier.py`, results in `results/kinematic/frontier/`, figure `frontier.png`
  (`scripts/plot_frontier.py`).

## Why the trade-off is physical
If release comes Δt late, the release direction has rotated by ω·Δt, and the length a ball travels
is more sensitive to elevation the faster it goes (∝ v²), so the landing error grows with v²·ω, which is
about v³. Measured on our bowlers (`results/kinematic/equifinal_mechanism.json`): the elevation term grows
as speed^2.86.

The only way to cancel it is to release later at a higher speed (Müller & Loosch's equifinal path),
which needs tangential hand acceleration at release:
- 50 km/h: ~115 m/s²
- 100 km/h: ~3000 m/s² (≈ 25×)

A fixed torque budget cannot supply that. So a fast delivery must either have very precise release
timing or accept a scatter in length.

## Limitations
- Open loop, one start, local optimiser: each frontier point is a lower bound on what is possible.
- The arm's speed limit is a model parameter (`arm_top_speed` = 40 rad/s damping); the 102 km/h delivery
  circles at 2245°/s, right at it.
- Every bowler, PPO and optimised, holds the elbow 18–22° hyperextended, past its −15° joint limit.
- One captured delivery (T3a) for the body.
