# Supervisor meeting, 20 Nov 2026

Notes from a Wispr Flow transcript of the meeting.

## What was presented
- **CMA-ES trajectory optimiser.** It finds the fastest legal, on-length delivery the arm can bowl. This is the ceiling for RL. The search is over the arm's joint torques only; the release angle is fixed per search.
- **Videos:**
  - 144 km/h without the arm handover. The arm runs free after about 1 s and does a slingshot or windmill swing, so it is not a cricket action.
  - 130 km/h with the handover at 0.5 s. It follows the motion capture until then, so the action is much more realistic.
- **RL from scratch** reached about 77 km/h. Started from the CMA-ES delivery, it reached 104 km/h, and 110 km/h in the new model.
- **Release-timing noise.** It models a real bowler's wobble at release: a timing error is sampled each episode and added to the release.

## Supervisor's questions and points
1. **How exactly is the CMA-ES output connected to PPO?** The supervisor listed two possibilities:
   - a reward term that rewards trajectories similar to the reference;
   - initialising the RL policy from the reference.

   Answer: **the second** (warm start, `scripts/bc_from_frontier.py`). PPO's actor is regressed onto the CMA-ES torques at recorded states; there is supervised learning on rollouts with small perturbations. The critic is regressed onto those rollouts' delivery rewards, and the exploration std is set low. PPO then fine-tunes the same network with the unchanged task reward (`envs/runup_bowl_env.py` `_task_reward`). There is **no** imitation term in the reward.
2. **Why do we need RL if CMA-ES already gives a policy?** Compare the CMA-ES policy alone against CMA-ES followed by RL, under the **same release-timing noise**.
3. **How is the elbow constraint handled in CMA-ES?** The same legality checker as RL. In the fitness function a violating delivery always ranks below every legal, on-length one, by how far it is over the limit. The search is guided, not brute force.
4. **Constrained RL** (from the last meeting) is still a direction to look into.
5. **Legs after the landing jump** look like sliding because the leg capture is poor there. Fix it **at the reference-motion level**: fill gaps by interpolating between the timestamps that do have data, and filter. Do not leave it to RL. In future recordings, make sure the legs are captured properly.
6. **Main next step: try an actual humanoid robot model.** Two sets of actuator and joint limits:
   - one human-like;
   - one from a real humanoid robot.

## Clarifications after the meeting (things said imprecisely)
- **The release angle is fixed, not the release time.** The ball leaves when the arm passes the set angle, so the time follows from the motion. The actions are only the joint torques, as the supervisor said.
- **CMA-ES does not search over release angles.** The angle is fixed per search (250, 270 or 290°).
- **Accuracy of the CMA-ES 130 km/h delivery under timing noise:** 39% at 1 ms, 21% at 2 ms, 11% at 4 ms, 5% at 8 ms and 4% at 12 ms. It is not 90% at 2 ms. The RL 110 km/h bowler is 74 / 47 / 25 / 13 / 9% at the same noise levels: slower than CMA-ES, but more accurate at every level.
- **The code is on GitHub** (public, last pushed 20 Nov).
