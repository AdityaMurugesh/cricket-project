# Supervisor meeting, 27 Nov 2026: prep

Main item: the supervisor's "main next step" from 20 Nov, **an actual humanoid robot model**. Until last week only the
bowling arm had robot limits, and the body was the captured human skeleton. Now **the whole bowler is a Unitree G1**.

Items marked **[HPC]** are filled in once pack `hpc/packs/g1_1.txt` has run (submit: `hpc\submit_g1.ps1`).

## 1. What was built
- **Robot model:** Unitree G1, 29 DoF, from MuJoCo Menagerie (`assets/unitree_g1`, BSD-3 Unitree licence).
  Unitree's own masses, link lengths, joint ranges and peak torques.
- **Capture → robot (retargeting)**, `scripts/retarget_g1.py`:
  - per-frame inverse kinematics matching each segment's direction (thigh, shank, foot, upper arm, forearm, hand)
    and the pelvis/torso frames, within the G1's joint ranges;
  - the G1 is 0.737× the bowler (leg 0.624 vs 0.846 m); the timing is kept;
  - a 10 Hz zero-phase filter (without it the IK flipped branches: 126 rad/s shoulder-yaw spikes).
- **G1 bowling environment**, `envs/g1_bowl_env.py`:
  - the body follows the retargeted capture;
  - the right arm is the G1's own 7-joint arm on its own motors (25 N·m and 37 rad/s at shoulder and elbow,
    5 N·m and 22 rad/s at wrist pitch/yaw), with a DC torque–speed curve;
  - same elbow law (≤15° extension, G1 flexion = 90° − q), length, line, drag and timing noise as before;
  - so CMA-ES, behaviour cloning + PPO, scoring and the videos all run on it unchanged (`--robot g1`).
- **Feasibility check**, `scripts/g1_feasibility.py`: could the G1 itself make the body motion it is given?

**Message:** the G1 is fast enough but not strong enough to bowl like the human. Its arm can't copy the human arm, so
the optimiser has to find the G1's own action. The whole-body bottleneck is the **waist** (2–5× its torque around
release), not the arm. The legs aren't assessed yet: their torques depend on ground forces (next step).

## 3. G1 pace/accuracy frontier (CMA-ES, HPC 23 Nov, job 43474 on the H200 node)
The fastest legal, on-length delivery the G1 can bowl, then the fastest that lands on length with probability P under
release-timing noise (τ = 0.5, 0.75). Every row is legal under the 15° elbow law.
`results/kinematic/frontier/g1/`, ~2 h for all six runs.

| run | fastest (τ=0) | τ=0.5: km/h (P on length) | τ=0.75: km/h (P) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 270°, timing SD 4 ms | **70.9** | 54.8 (0.58) | 48.5 (0.78) |
| 250°, 4 ms | 77.2 | 54.4 (0.51) | 48.7 (0.75) |
| 290°, 4 ms | 67.2 | 58.3 (0.55) | 54.9 (0.82) |
| 270°, 12 ms (less skilled) | 70.9 | 47.0 (0.66) | 44.1 (0.77) |
| 270°, 4 ms, arm motors ×3 (≈ human arm strength) | **88.2** | 57.5 (0.58) | 53.1 (0.89) |
| 270°, 4 ms, arm free from the start (no captured swing) | 76.6 | 53.7 (0.50) | 54.7 (0.75) |

For comparison:
- Human arm motion replayed on the G1: 46 km/h, 1.8 m off line.
- Human-limits arm on the captured body: 130 km/h (same settings, 270°, handover 0.5 s).

**Reading:**
- The G1 bowls about half as fast as the human-limits model: 71 vs 130 km/h.
- Tripling its arm motors only gets it to 88 km/h. So most of the gap isn't motor strength: it is the
  smaller body (0.74×) and the G1's arm and waist ranges.
- Freeing the arm from the captured swing gains about 6 km/h at full pace. It is the most accurate at a given pace:
  54.7 km/h at P = 0.75, against 48.5 with the human swing.
- Accuracy costs speed as before: about 70 → 55 km/h for a 50–60 % on-length bowler.

Figure: `results/kinematic/cmaes_vs_rl/g1_cmaes_vs_rl.png`. Videos (gitignored): `results/kinematic/videos/kin2_g1_*.mp4`.

**Reading:** the 20 Nov result carries over to the robot. Under motor noise the plan loses its good balls and RL keeps
them: 1.5–4× more at 15 %. Four seeds agree within 1.5 km/h. For the fastest balls RL gives up 6–10 km/h to double the
accuracy. Arm-free-from-start: the open-loop plan fails at every other start time (0 %), while RL bowls 42 km/h, 57 %.
Caveat: without motor noise part of RL's gain is pace it gives up.

## 3c. First DYNAMIC whole-body G1 test (27 Nov, laptop: `scripts/g1_dynamic.py`)
The whole G1 physically simulated: floor contact, gravity, Unitree's own servos with URDF torque limits tracking the retargeted delivery.
- The retarget's feet were **7 cm under the floor** (max 11), and the planted foot slid at ~2 m/s. New `retarget_g1.py --feet --froude`
  (reference `T3aLf`) solves the pelvis, pins planted feet, and Froude-scales time (×√0.737 = 0.86): slip 0.6 m/s, feet on the floor.
- **Servos alone: falls** in 0.15–0.45 s (trunk past 60°). A virtual pelvis spring gets it through but needs ~1.6–2.1× body weight
  (p95). Bowling arm and waist at their torque limits most of the time.
- Figure `results/kinematic/g1/dynamic_T3aLf.png`; videos `g1_dynamic_fixed_nohelp.mp4`, `g1_dynamic_fixed_help3000.mp4`.
- So a learned balance/tracking controller is needed: DeepMimic-style tracking RL overnight (`envs/g1_track_env.py`,
  `scripts/train_g1_track.py`; PPO adds up to ±0.25/±0.5 rad to every servo target every 20 ms; random start points; a fall
  ends the episode). HPC jobs 47153/47156/47157, 3 runs × 8.5 h on 16 CPUs.

## 3d. Overnight result: the RL controller keeps the physical G1 up through the whole delivery
- At 25M steps (~3 h), **all 3 runs carry the G1 from 0.94 s before release (run-up at 3+ m/s) through the bound,
  landing, release and follow-through without falling** (64 steps, 1.28 s; joints ~8–11° from the reference, pelvis
  0.12–0.2 m). Servos alone fell in 0.15 s.
- From the clip's very first frame it still falls (~0.3 s in): the retargeted pelvis whips ~21° in the first 40 ms
  (13 rad/s), a retarget edge artefact.
- Started every 60 ms through the clip it finishes from 15–16 of 21 points (`scripts/eval_g1_track_starts.py`).
  Practice runs reaching the end: 45% at 5M steps → 68–82% at 25M, still rising; ±0.5 rad corrections best.
- **Catch:** the bowling hand reaches ~33–37 km/h at release against ~50 km/h in the reference (wrist, 20 ms grid): it
  keeps its balance by giving up arm speed (the G1 arm motors are ~3× too weak, sec. 3). No ball or legality yet in
  this whole-body version.
- Figure `results/kinematic/g1/track_summary.png`; video `results/kinematic/videos/g1track_a050_lr1e4_s2_k3.mp4`
  (laptop). The runs continue to ~70M steps (ends ~05:45 UTC).

## 4. Likely questions
- *Is the body physically simulated?* In the bowling results (sec. 3, 3b), no: the body is prescribed and only the arm is
  torque-driven. Sec. 3c is the first fully dynamic test: with servos alone the G1 falls within half a second; with the
  overnight RL tracking controller (3d) it gets through the delivery on its own feet, but there's no ball in that version yet.
- *Why does the G1 bowl slower than the human-limit model?* Its arm motors are 25 N·m. The ×3 run shows how much of
  the gap is motor strength and how much is size (0.74×).
- *Two sets of limits (human-like vs real robot)?* Real robot = G1 body + G1 motors. Human-like = the earlier
  human-limits arm, or the G1 with ×3 arm motors.
